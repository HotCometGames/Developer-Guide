# What Are Hackathons & Game Jams?

> Time-constrained creative events where you build something from scratch — and the unique pressure and energy that comes with them.

> **Related:** [Choosing an Idea](choosing-an-idea.md) | [Team Formation & Roles](team-formation-and-roles.md)

---

## What Are They?

A **hackathon** (hack + marathon) is a time-limited event where participants build software projects — web apps, APIs, mobile apps, hardware hacks, or anything in between. Teams form around ideas and race to ship something demo-worthy.

A **game jam** is the same concept but focused on making a **game** — often tied to a theme that's announced at the start.

| | Hackathon | Game Jam |
|--|-----------|----------|
| **Goal** | Build a working product or prototype | Make a playable game |
| **Duration** | 24-48 hours typical | 48-72 hours typical (sometimes longer) |
| **Theme** | Often open or a broad topic | Specific theme announced at kickoff |
| **Output** | Working demo + pitch | Playable game + itch.io page |
| **Judging** | Business value, technical execution, design | Fun, creativity, theme interpretation |
| **Typical size** | 100-1000+ participants | 50-5000+ participants |
| **Platform** | DevPost, Eventbrite, company-internal | Itch.io (Ludum Dare, GMTK, Brackeys) |

## Why Participate?

| Reason | What You Get |
|--------|-------------|
| **Ship something fast** | Practice moving from idea to working software without getting stuck |
| **Learn new tech** | No time for perfectionism — you just figure it out |
| **Portfolio piece** | A shipped project beats a half-finished tutorial |
| **Meet people** | Find collaborators, friends, future co-founders |
| **Build discipline** | Timeboxing, scoping, and tradeoff skills transfer to day jobs |
| **It's fun** | The energy is addictive. You'll surprise yourself. |

## Types of Events

### By Format

| Format | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| **In-person** | High energy, instant collaboration, swag | Travel, limited to local events |
| **Online** | Global participation, work in your space | Lonelier, harder to coordinate |
| **Hybrid** | Both options | Logistics complexity |

### By Sponsor

| Type | Example | Focus |
|------|---------|-------|
| **Community** | Ludum Dare, Global Game Jam, HackMIT | Creativity, open-ended |
| **Company** | Github Hackathon, internal company events | Recruitment, internal innovation |
| **Themed** | AI Hackathon, Climate Hackathon | Specific technology or cause |

> **Note:** This chapter focuses on community and competitive events. Company hackathons have similar dynamics but include internal politics and business alignment.

## The Typical Timeline

```
Before the event
├── Register (weeks before)
├── Form team (optional — can find at event)
├── Pre-setup environment, install tools
└── Get good sleep

During the event
├── Kickoff — theme announced, ideas generated
├── Team formation
├── Building (the bulk of the time)
├── Midpoint check-in
├── Final push — bug fixes, polish
├── Submissions close
└── Demos and judging

After the event
├── Play others' entries
├── Write a post-mortem
├── Rest
└── Optional: continue the project
```

## Common Fears (And Why They're Wrong)

| Fear | Reality |
|------|---------|
| "I'm not good enough" | Every level is welcome. You're there to learn. |
| "I won't finish" | Almost nobody finishes everything they planned. That's fine. |
| "My idea is stupid" | Execution matters more than the idea. Ship it and prove yourself wrong. |
| "I don't know anyone" | That's why you go. Solo is viable too. |
| "I'll embarrass myself" | Everyone is too focused on their own project to judge yours. |
