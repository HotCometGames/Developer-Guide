# The Pitch & Demo

> How to present your project in 60-120 seconds — the hook, the demo, the impact, and how to handle everything going wrong.

> **Related:** [Polish & Juice](polish-and-juice.md) | [Building the MVP](building-the-mvp.md)

---

## What Is It?

The pitch is your chance to tell judges what you built, why it matters, and show it working. Most pitches are 60-120 seconds. You don't have time to explain everything — you have time to make people **care**.

## The 60-Second Pitch Structure

| Time | Section | Content |
|------|---------|---------|
| 0-10s | **Hook** | Grab attention. "Have you ever..." or a surprising stat. |
| 10-25s | **Problem** | What pain point does this solve? Why should we care? |
| 25-40s | **Solution** | What did you build? Mention the tech if relevant. |
| 40-50s | **Demo** | Show it working. Live or recorded. |
| 50-60s | **Impact** | Why does this matter? What would you do with more time? |

### Example Script

```
(Hook)
"Have you ever spent 20 minutes digging through Slack to find that one
decision from last week? We built Decision Tracker so you never have to."

(Problem)
"Teams make hundreds of decisions every sprint, but they're scattered
across DMs, threads, and meeting notes. Nobody can find them later."

(Solution)
"Decision Tracker hooks into Slack and GitHub. Every time someone makes
a decision, it logs it with context — who decided, when, and why."

(Demo)
"Here's the dashboard. You can see all decisions for this sprint,
filtered by team. Click one and you get the full thread context.
We've indexed 500 decisions in the last 30 minutes."

(Impact)
"We think this saves each developer 2 hours per week. With more time,
we'd add automatic Slack summaries and a search across all teams."
```

## The Demo

### Live Demo Best Practices

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Have a **clear script** of what you'll click | Fumble through UI trying to find it |
| Use **realistic data** ("Alice's Order #42") | Show "foo", "bar", "test" |
| **Practice the flow** 2-3 times before presenting | First time in front of judges |
| Show the **core feature** — the one thing it does | Try to show every feature |
| Have a **backup recording** ready | Rely entirely on a live demo |

### The Backup Recording

Record a 60-second demo video of your project working perfectly. If the live demo fails, play the video.

> **Tip:** Record the video immediately after your MVP is stable. You can always re-record if you add polish. Having a backup removes the demo-day anxiety.

### When the Demo Fails (And It Will)

| Failure | Recovery |
|---------|----------|
| App won't load | "We're having a deployment issue. Here's the video." → Play backup |
| Feature doesn't work | "This was working 10 minutes ago — we hit a last-minute bug. What I'd show you is..." → Describe it with confidence |
| Internet is down | "Fortunately, we recorded a demo." → Play backup |
| Forgot what to say | "The gist is..." → Keep talking. The audience doesn't know your script. |

> **Remember:** Judges have seen hundreds of demos. They know things break. How you handle it matters more than the break itself.

## The Presentation

### Slides (If Allowed)

Keep it minimal. Most hackathons prefer live demos over slides, but if you use slides:

| Slide | Content |
|-------|---------|
| 1 | Title + team names (10 seconds) |
| 2 | Problem — what and why (15 seconds) |
| 3 | Solution — what you built (15 seconds) |
| 4 | Live demo OR recorded video (30 seconds) |
| 5 | Impact + future (10 seconds) |

> **Rule:** One slide per 15 seconds of presentation time. No walls of text.

### Non-Verbal

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Make eye contact (or look at the camera if remote) | Read from your laptop screen |
| Speak clearly and slow down | Rush through to fit everything in |
| Sound excited — if you're not excited, why should they be? | Monotone delivery |
| Dress like a human (doesn't need to be formal) | Wear pajamas (yes, people have done this) |

## For Game Jams: The Trailer Format

Game jam presentations often work better as a **trailer**:

| 0-5s | Game title + your name |
| 5-20s | Show the core gameplay loop |
| 20-35s | Show a unique mechanic or "the twist" |
| 35-45s | Show juice, art, audio |
| 45-50s | Theme connection — how it ties in |
| 50-60s | "Available on itch.io" |

> **Tip:** A recording with music and gameplay footage is better than a live demo for games. The jam organizers will thank you.

## Q&A

After your pitch, judges will ask questions. Common ones:

| Question | How to Answer |
|----------|---------------|
| "What was the hardest part?" | Be honest about a real technical challenge and how you solved it |
| "What would you add next?" | Have an answer ready — shows you've thought about the future |
| "How is this different from X?" | Acknowledge X exists, then explain your unique angle |
| "Who's your target user?" | Be specific — "freelance developers" not "everyone" |
| "What tech did you use?" | Mention 2-3 key choices and why (not a laundry list) |

### Q&A Survival Rules

1. **If you don't know, say so.** "That's a great question — we haven't thought about that. I'd explore [direction]."
2. **Don't BS.** Judges can smell it. Say "I don't know" with confidence.
3. **Your whole team can answer.** Pass the question to the person who knows.
4. **Keep it short.** Answer the question, then stop talking.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Problem | Solution |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Demo-first, no hook | Nobody knows why they should care | Start with the problem |
| Too much tech detail | Judges' eyes glaze over | Focus on user value, not architecture |
| Reading from slides | Looks unprepared | Know your opening and closing lines |
| No backup demo | One bug = no demo | Record a video before presenting |
| Running over time | Judges cut you off | Time your pitch. Aim for 80% of the limit. |
| Taking up all Q&A time with one person | Others don't get to ask | Short answers, then ask "any other questions?" |
